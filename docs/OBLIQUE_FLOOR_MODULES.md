# Oblique floor tiles for cell, corridor, and canteen

The existing Blender environment catalog already contains `floor.cell.sealed-concrete`, `floor.linoleum.institutional` (the corridor floor), and `floor.canteen.terrazzo`. This batch isolates each authored one-tile collection at its source origin and renders it into the shared camera grid: 24 yaw positions, 15° apart, at elevations 25°, 45°, and 65°. Each transparent frame is 512 × 512, orthographic 64 px/tile, with the tile center at pivot `(256, 256)`. The material palette remains the existing catalog palette; the canteen's warm pink terrazzo matches its current overhead asset.

The three logical IDs are registered for the common nearest-pose selector and lazy Phaser texture loader. Full HD Chromium preview requested only visited frames and retained previously loaded frames in cache. At 1920 × 1080, five yaw values at both low and high elevation loaded 11 cell frames (154,365 bytes, including the initial selected pose), 10 corridor frames (110,485 bytes), and 10 canteen frames (105,062 bytes). The selected pivot stayed at `(1370, 585)` under the pointer. All images returned HTTP 200. Captures at yaw -45°, 0°, and 45° at both elevations are generated in the system temporary directory by `tooling/qa-oblique-floor-browser.mjs`. At native scale, the cell aggregate, cool corridor linoleum, and warm canteen terrazzo remain visually distinct.

All 216 PNGs and three manifests reproduced identical SHA-256 hashes on a second Blender 5.2.1 render. Catalog sizes: cell 1,085,675 bytes; corridor 879,094 bytes; canteen 832,582 bytes. The registry contract failed before their manifests existed and passes after rendering. The pinned Blender live determinism gate passes as well.

These are modular assets ready for the separate ObliqueWorldScene painter. This branch does not change its ground draw path, camera/projector, HUD, or gameplay. The painter must select these module IDs from the real room/terrain state before the tiles become visible in game.
