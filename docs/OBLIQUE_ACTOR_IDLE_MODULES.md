# Prisoner and guard in the rotating view

The fixed-camera actor atlases encode eight **world movement** directions. Their labels do not change when the camera yaws, and their frames were rendered for one fixed camera. Reusing an atlas frame directly in the oblique scene would therefore keep the old viewing angle as the room rotates. The current oblique painter draws actors as orange geometry for that reason.

This batch renders the existing original Blender prisoner and guard models as idle poses for the oblique view. Each role has 24 yaw poses at 15° steps and elevations 25°, 45°, and 65°. The source model's ground-anchored `SpriteRoot` is scaled to 0.5, yielding an approximately one-and-a-half-tile standing figure next to the wall module. The transparent render uses the common 512 × 512 frame, 64 px/tile, world-origin foot pivot `(256, 256)`, and the shared nearest-pose/lazy texture loader. Existing atlas animation remains the source for the fixed-camera view.

Blender 5.2.1 reproduced all 144 PNGs and both manifests with identical SHA-256 hashes. Full catalog size is 910,997 bytes for the prisoner and 940,316 bytes for the guard. At 1920 × 1080, the Phaser preview loaded 11 selected prisoner images (136,155 bytes, including the initial pose) and 10 guard images (125,776 bytes) while checking five yaw values at both 25° and 65°. All responses were HTTP 200, and the foot pivot remained at `(1370, 585)` under the pointer. Captures for yaw -45°, 0°, and 45° at low and high elevation are produced by `tooling/qa-oblique-actor-browser.mjs`. At native scale, orange jumpsuit and navy cap/vest distinguish the roles.

The registry contract failed before the manifests existed and passes after rendering. The pinned Blender live determinism gate passes. The separate ObliqueWorldScene branch must select these actor IDs and place the foot pivot at each projected actor position. This PR does not change that scene, camera, HUD, or gameplay. Walking and incident animation across changing yaw remain a later art and painter integration step.
