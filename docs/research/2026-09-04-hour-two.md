## The verdict, in one paragraph

**Hour two is five minutes long, and the thing that breaks in it is not the
economy — it is the presses.** A prison of 17 to 30 residents run across
seventeen in-game days is *arithmetically perfect and completely inert*: the
treasury credits `300 × residents − 80 × guards` at every day boundary, to the
minor unit, and act 4's ledger reads **4,780 at seven consecutive boundaries**
while act 3's reads **6,060 at four**, riot days included. Nothing grows without
bound (1,063 → 1,088 HUD nodes over 17 days, no fold hiding anything), nothing
runs out, nothing can go wrong, and the treasury triples with nothing to spend
it on. What *does* go wrong is the act of growing: **a run of presses loses
some of them.** Seven of 25 *Admit* presses in act 4 and five of eight *Hire
Guard* presses in act 3 were refused before they reached the worker, each with
`"The simulation has not reported its command sequence yet; try again in a
moment."` in the console and a refusal sentence on screen that says only that
the request was refused — so that prison spent seven in-game days with 17
residents instead of 24 (30% of its income) and three guards instead of eight,
and **every one of the 15 incidents across those two acts ran out of time
instead of being contained** while the Security panel said *"This prison has
the guards it asks for."* Meanwhile the people rot in plain view — day 13's
roster reads `Hunger 0%` and `Hygiene 4%` — and the money does not notice,
because the withholding rate is suspended at `0`. That is #503 inverted: it
measured a grant eroding in silence; at v0.0.451 (`0e614c7`) the erosion is
gone and the silence is not.

**Is there anything to do?** Between one built prison and a bigger one the
game asks for exactly two things: more beds, and more guards. Both are
quantities, neither is a decision, and one of them (guards) has no observable
effect a player can find. **Does it hold?** Yes, completely — at 30 residents
and 17 days nothing leaks, overflows or stops being legible. **Does it get
better or worse?** Neither. Day 16's ledger line *is* day 10's ledger line.

This is the finding. Everything else in this record is a property of a prison
standing still; this is the only thing that goes wrong while a player is
*playing*, and growing a prison is nothing but runs of presses.

**MEASURED, act 4.** 25 presses of *Admit a prisoner* into a 17-bed cell, one
every 120 ms, on a clock at 4×. The page console carried this **seven times**:

### The mechanism, read rather than guessed

**VERIFIED, read**, five files:

- `src/ui/simulation-commands.ts:251-253` — `submit` throws
  `'The simulation has not reported its command sequence yet; try again in a
  moment.'` when `!this.sequenceSynced`. That is the exact string above.
- `src/ui/simulation-commands.ts:327` — *"A rejection means our idea of the
  sequence is wrong in an unknown direction. Drop the baseline and let the next
  snapshot restate it."* `if (message.payload.status === 'rejected')
  this.sequenceSynced = false;`
- `src/simulation/worker/state-machine.ts:1147` — the only producer of
  `status: 'rejected'`, from a throw out of `Kernel.submitCommand`.
- `src/simulation/kernel/kernel.ts:142-146` — that throw:
  `if (executeAtTick < this._tick) throw new CommandRejectedError('past-tick', …)`.
- `src/ui/simulation-commands.ts:77` —
  `const DEFAULT_LEAD_TICKS = 20; // 1 second at the kernel's 20 Hz.`

**REASONED, and the arithmetic is the finding.** `projectFromClock`
(`simulation-commands.ts:190-195`) estimates the worker's tick from elapsed
real time *scaled by speed*, then adds a flat 20-tick lead. The kernel steps
every 50 ms per tick (`FixedStepClock`'s `stepMilliseconds = 50`,
`src/simulation/clock/fixed-step-clock.ts:29`) and the speed ladder tops out at
4× (`SIMULATION_SPEEDS = [1, 2, 4]`, `:17`), so at maximum speed the kernel
runs **80 ticks a second** and a 20-tick lead is **250 ms of real time, not the
one second the comment claims.** One main-thread stall longer than that — a
repaint, a GC, a loaded machine — puts the next command's `executeAtTick`
behind the kernel's tick, the worker rejects it as `past-tick`, the sender
drops its baseline, and **every press after it is refused until the next
`simulation/snapshot` restates the sequence.** The safety margin shrinks
exactly as the clock speeds up, and §11 shows the clock is why a player would
be at 4× at all.

### What the player is told

**VERIFIED, read.** `reportError` (`src/ui/hud/hud.ts:1365-1381`) does put a
sentence on the refusal line for these two actions — `hud.refusal.hire-staff`
and `hud.refusal.admit-prisoner` are both keyed
(`src/ui/hud/messages.ts:1203`, `:1266`) — and the sentences are:

```
'hud.refusal.hire-staff':      'Nobody was hired — the request was refused and no money was spent.'
'hud.refusal.admit-prisoner':  'Nobody was admitted — the request was refused.'
```

(`src/content/default-locale-en.ts:1869`, `:1898`.) Both are **true** and
neither is **useful**: the real answer is *try again in a moment*, and that
half reaches `console.warn` only. **This is the weakest link in this finding
and §14 says so** — I never captured `.hud__refusal` at the instant of a
dropped press, only the keys and the render path.

**What it costs a player.** Act 4's prison ran days 10-17 with 17 residents
where the player had asked for 25: **2,100 a day less income, about 30% of the
prison's earnings**, for seven in-game days, with nothing on any panel saying
a number is short of what was requested. Act 3's ran with three guards where
the player had asked for eight, which is §2. **JUDGEMENT:** a player who
presses *Admit* twenty-five times and counts seventeen prisoners will conclude
the prison has a cap, not that their presses were dropped.

**Not in `docs/research/`, and not #503.** Nothing in this directory names a
lost run of presses; the nearest thing is the withdrawn-findings hazard the
briefs warn about, which is about a *tester's* presses landing on the HUD, not
about a player's landing and being refused.

**Caveat, stated as strongly as it needs to be.** This container ran five
parallel testers with several browsers, so **the rate — 7 in 25, 5 in 8 — is
not a claim about a player's machine.** What is not about this box is the
250 ms margin, the `past-tick` rejection, the baseline drop and the two
sentences: those are read from the code and hold everywhere.

### Why the panel's number is the wrong number

**VERIFIED, read**, four values:

- Guards to **post** = `max(scheduled, ceil(occupants / 8))` —
  `resolveOccupancyScaledGuardCount`,
  `src/simulation/security/sector-staffing.ts:190`, with
  `DEFAULT_SECTOR_PRISONERS_PER_GUARD = 8` at `:147` and the floor of 1 at
  `src/simulation/security/default-sector.ts:113`. At 17 residents that is 3;
  at 30 it is 4. **The panel's number is right about posting.**
- Guards to **respond** = `max(1, ceil(severity × 0.5))` —
  `requiredResponderCount`, `src/simulation/incidents/response-system.ts:345`,
  with `respondersPerSeverityPoint: 0.5` at `:26`.
- An assault's severity is scaled into `[1, ASSAULT_SEVERITY_CEILING]` =
  `[1, 5]` — `src/simulation/incidents/trigger-system.ts:444`,
  `src/simulation/incidents/flashpoint.ts:373`. So an assault of severity 3 or
  more needs **two** responders and one of severity 5 needs three.
- Responders are drawn from *claimable* guards, and a posted guard is not one —
  `claimableResponders`, `response-system.ts:499-512`: *"only a post-eligible
  role counts towards `required`"*, and `available.length < required` returns
  `undefined`, i.e. **no dispatch at all**.

**REASONED.** At 17 residents the floor is 3 posted **plus** up to 3 free, so
five or six guards, and the panel says three. Act 4 hired four — one free — and
every assault of severity ≥ 3 therefore had nobody to send. Act 3 hired three,
none free, and nothing could be sent to anything.

**The code already says this in words; the panel does not.**
`src/simulation/security/default-sector.ts:100-112`, verbatim:

> a prison of `n` prisoners needs `ceil(n / 8)` guards posted **plus** a
> reserve for `IncidentResponseSystem` to claim, and **a player who hires
> exactly the requirement will watch every incident lapse.**

That is a prediction, and acts 3 and 4 are it happening.

**Extends `2026-09-04-does-anyone-answer-an-incident.md`, and does not
re-report it.** That record measured the two ends of the same axis on an
8-prisoner, 1-bed prison: **six guards → 15 of 15 resolved, zero injuries**;
**zero guards → 19 of 19 lapsed, 114 injuries, three escapes**. It did not
measure between them, and it did not measure a *housed* prison at scale. These
two acts are the middle: **4 guards / 17 residents → 7 of 7 lapsed**, **3
guards / 30 residents → 8 of 8 lapsed**, both with the population fully or
mostly in beds. Its finding that the screen cannot be asked which ending an
incident had is unchanged and not re-filed.

**What it costs a player.** The entire security half of the game does nothing,
and the panel whose job is to say so says *"This prison has the guards it asks
for."* **JUDGEMENT:** a player who reads that sentence and stops hiring has
been told the opposite of what they need. Combined with §1, act 3's player
asked for eight guards, got three, and was told three was one short — and the
truthful number was six.

## 4. #503 INVERTED — the people get worse in plain view and the money no longer notices

Issue [#503](https://github.com/matmaxalez/lockstate/issues/503) measured, at
v0.0.163, a correct prison's grant eroding `300 → 260 → 220 → 180` over four
days and named the gap: *"the player learns that they are paid less and not
why."* **At v0.0.451 (`0e614c7`) there is nothing left to learn.**

**MEASURED, act 1, the Regime tab at day 13** — the whole roster block, four
rows of twenty-four:

```
PRISONERS
4 of 24
Lars Tamm       Medium   Association   Hygiene   4%
Carla Rossi     Medium   Idle          Hunger    0%
Rafal Eriksen   Medium   Idle          Hunger    0%
Ibrahim Xavier  Low      Association   Hygiene   6%
and 20 more
```

**MEASURED, the same day, the same screen:** `33,290 · FUNDS`,
`0 · HIGH RISK`, and act 1's day-11 boundary credited `3,600` gross — the full
rate, for people whose worst need reads `0%`.

**VERIFIED, read**, and this is the measured decay table the code carries about
itself (`src/simulation/prisoners/sentence.ts:92-113`):

| need | rate | first unmet tick | in days |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| bladder | 0.08 | 2,550 | 1.06 |
| hunger | 0.05 | 4,080 | 1.70 |
| sleep | 0.03 | 6,790 | 2.83 |
| **hygiene** | 0.02 | **10,180** | **4.24** |
| **recreation** | 0.015 | **13,570** | **5.65** |
| safety | 0.01 | 20,360 | 8.48 |

The two in bold are **room-gated** by ADR 0054 decision 1: *"a prison with no
shower room or laundry cannot serve `hygiene`, and one with no yard, common
room or classroom cannot serve `recreation`"*, and *"the other four are served
by a furnished cell **and a canteen**."* Every prison in this record has one
room — a cell with beds and a toilet — so **hunger, hygiene and recreation are
all unservable in it**, which is exactly what the roster shows.

**REASONED.** At the shipped withholding rate of `40`, act 4's prison would
have earned `300 − 3 × 40 = 180` per resident-day rather than 300 — **3,060
gross rather than 5,100, a 40% cut**, every day from day 12 onward. At the
suspended rate of `0` the credited figure is `4,780` on every one of those
days, and `stateIncomeAccruedTodayMinorUnits` just before each boundary reads
5,057 / 5,074 / 5,021 / 5,038 / 5,057 / 5,032 / 5,080 — about 297 per resident,
i.e. nothing withheld at all.

**So #503's first half has inverted rather than been fixed.** Its measurement
was *money falling in silence*. Mine is *money flat in silence, over people the
roster reports at 0%*. The player still has no feedback loop — and now they do
not even have the falling number that was the only signal #503 credited them
with. **JUDGEMENT:** this is the more dangerous of the two states, because a
flat line reads as "nothing is wrong" while a falling one at least reads as
"something is happening".

**And the cause is visible for one sixth of the population.** `rosterRows` was
**4** at every sample in every act, at 17, 24 and 30 prisoners.
**VERIFIED, read:** `PRISONER_ROSTER_ROW_LIMIT = 4`
(`src/ui/hud/regime-panel.ts:293`), which is a deliberate, owner-ruled budget —
the fourth ruling of 2026-08-31 kept it at four and changed *which* four
(highest risk tier first). Not a defect, and worth stating anyway: in these
prisons `prisonersHighRisk` was `0` at every sample, so the tier ordering had
nothing to sort by and the four rows are an arbitrary window on twenty-six
people whose needs are all at the floor.

## 7. MEASURED — nothing grows without bound, and after day 14 nothing arrives at all

The brief asks whether a queue, an alert band, a list or a number grows without
bound at 20-30 residents. **Measured over 17 in-game days: no, none of them**,
and the reason is that the volume of everything is bounded by design.

**Act 4's DOM, sampled every ~25 s through the whole hands-off run:**

| tick | day | alertRows | rosterRows | blockRows | staffHeldRows | queueRows | deliveryRows | roomRows | hudNodes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 24,509 | 11 | 3 | 4 | 0 | 6 | 3 | 3 | 18 | **1,063** |
| 25,139 | 11 | 3 | 4 | 2 | 6 | 3 | 3 | 18 | 1,073 |
| 29,352 | 13 | 3 | 4 | 2 | 6 | 3 | 3 | 18 | 1,073 |
| 33,611 | 15 | 4 | 4 | 2 | 6 | 3 | 3 | 18 | 1,088 |
| 39,906 | 17 | 4 | 4 | 2 | 6 | 3 | 3 | 18 | **1,088** |

**Twenty-five nodes in seventeen in-game days**, and every list is a fixed
window: `FINAL folds: []` — not one scroll container in the HUD was hiding any
of its own content at the end of act 4.

**And the alerts column froze.** From the day-15 sample onward it is
*byte-identical* at four consecutive readings 2,000+ ticks apart, every row
stamped `Day 14`:

```
A fight has broken out between two prisoners. 7× Day 14 / Warning / Clear this alert
No incident is still open — but the last one ran out of time … 7× Day 14 / Warning / Clear this alert
Contraband found: Tool. Day 14 / Warning / Clear this alert
Nothing was removed — there is no object on that tile, and none being built there. / Warning
```

**Nothing new arrived in the last three in-game days of the run** — no
incident, no contraband, no intake, no bill. The `INCIDENTS` chip read
`0 · Clear` at every one of those samples. **I do not know why**, and I am
marking that: the fight counter stopped at `7×` on day 14 and the run went on
to day 17. What would establish the cause is the incident log itself, which
`2026-09-04-does-anyone-answer-an-incident.md` reaches through a projection
this instrument does not request.

**Why nothing could grow, VERIFIED by reading:**

- The alerts list is capped at **8** rows — `MAX_EVENT_ALERT_ROWS = 8`,
  `src/ui/simulation-events.ts:447`, with severity-ordered eviction. Act 4
  peaked at 4 and act 3 at 4.
- Incident volume is capped **per sector**: one riot or gang retaliation per
  `DEFAULT_SECTOR_QUIET_TICKS_AFTER_INCIDENT` (4,800 ticks, two in-game days),
  one assault per 2,400, one escape attempt per 12,000 —
  `src/simulation/incidents/trigger-system.ts:84`, `:125`, `:136`, `:476-484`,
  whose own comment says *"This is where the volume is bounded, not at
  `MAX_EVENT_ALERT_ROWS`."*
- **And there is exactly one sector, and it is the whole prison.**
  `applyDefaultSecuritySector` is called once per session
  (`src/simulation/runtime/new-session.ts:1187`) and ADR 0048's rule, quoted at
  `:1190-1194`, is *"the derived default sector is the prison, so its occupants
  are every prisoner standing on owned land."*

**REASONED, and it is the structural half of the brief's third question:
a 30-resident prison generates the same volume of events as a 1-resident one.**
The quiet windows are per `(sector, kind)` and there is one sector, so growing
the prison from 1 to 30 residents multiplies the income by 30 and the number of
things that happen by **one**. That is why day 16 reads like day 10.

## 8. MEASURED — construction is not the wait, and this record's own first reading of it was wrong

Acts 1, 3 and 4 all reached a furnished, populated, staffed prison at around
**in-game day 11**, having started on day 1, and my first reading of that was
that building a prison costs nine in-game days. **Act 6 refutes it**, and it is
recorded here rather than quietly dropped.

Act 6 draws the same 24-segment perimeter with the clock **paused**, so no
in-game time is spent on the drawing at all, then starts the clock and polls:

```
[act6] the 60 bricks landed by tick 261 (125 ticks after the clock started)
[act6] paused at tick 475, clock {"mode":"paused"}
[act6] 24 order(s) drawn while paused; the clock moved 0 tick(s) while drawing
[act6] queue with the clock stopped: "QUEUED 24 waiting · 0 being built"
[act6] tick 769  (+84):   "QUEUED 20 waiting · 1 being built"
[act6] tick 1055 (+370):  "QUEUED 16 waiting · 1 being built"
[act6] tick 1343 (+658):  "QUEUED 11 waiting · 1 being built"
[act6] tick 1631 (+946):  "QUEUED 6 waiting · 0 being built"
[act6] tick 1920 (+1235): "QUEUED 1 waiting · 1 being built"
[act6] THE PERIMETER IS UP at tick 2104: 1419 ticks for 24 wall segments
       = 59.1 ticks each, 0.59 in-game days for the room
```

**59.1 ticks a wall, and the code predicts 60.** `wall-brick`'s
`workRequired` is 50 (`src/simulation/construction/definition.ts:88`),
`ConstructionSystem` advances `order.progress += 10` per scheduled tick
(`src/simulation/construction/system.ts:1316`) on
`schedule = { intervalTicks: 10, phaseTicks: 0 }` (`:210`), and the crew is
**one worker, always** — `MOCK_CREW_WORKER_ID` (`:203`, assigned at `:1310`)
under a docblock that says *"one order in progress at a time"* (`:1142`). Five
progress ticks plus one promotion tick, single-file, is 60 ticks a wall.
`grep -rn MOCK_CREW_WORKER_ID src/` returns **three lines, all in that one
file**, so nothing a player can buy adds a second builder — but at 60 ticks a
segment nothing needs to.

**So where did nine in-game days go?** Into this instrument's own round trips.
The clock runs at 4× throughout a build, and a loaded box makes every
`page.evaluate`, every `topAt`, every `locator.click` cost tens or hundreds of
simulation ticks. That is a fact about the harness — **and the general form of
it is a real fact about the game**, which is §11.

## 11. MEASURED and VERIFIED — one in-game day is thirty real seconds, and hour two is therefore about 120 in-game days

**VERIFIED, read:** `SIMULATION_SPEEDS = [1, 2, 4]`
(`src/simulation/clock/fixed-step-clock.ts:17`) and
`stepMilliseconds = 50` (`:29`), so the maximum speed a player can select runs
**80 ticks a second** and `DAY_LENGTH_TICKS` (2,400) is **30 real seconds**.

**MEASURED, act 4:** the hands-off run walked tick 24,276 → 40,636 — 16,360
ticks, 6.8 in-game days — inside a 195-second budget. Act 3 covered 40,481
ticks in a 10-minute act including its build.

**REASONED, and this is the answer to the brief's framing.** The question asks
what *the next hour of play* consists of. At maximum speed an hour of play is
about **120 in-game days**, and §3 and §7 have measured that days 10 through 17
are one line repeated. So the honest answer is not that hour two is empty for
an hour — it is that **hour two is over in five minutes**, after which the
player is watching a constant. **JUDGEMENT:** and the fast clock is exactly
what makes §1 bite, because the sender's 20-tick safety margin is 250 ms at 4×
and one second at 1×. A player who slows down to keep their presses stops the
only thing that was happening.

## 14. My weakest claim, and what would change my mind

**The weakest claim is §1's player-facing half — that the sentence a dropped
press produces does not tell the player to try again.** The mechanism is
MEASURED (seven and four console lines, quoted) and VERIFIED (five files, each
opened at the line cited), and the two sentences are quoted from the shipped
locale. What I **never captured** is `.hud__refusal` at the instant of a
dropped press: act 4 predates that instrumentation and act 3's post-admit read
of the band returned the calibration probe's own stale `RemoveObject` message,
because its admissions all succeeded. So *"the player sees `Nobody was hired —
the request was refused and no money was spent.`"* is a claim about the render
path (`reportError` → `refusalMessageKey` → `refusalText.textContent`) rather
than about a screen I read.

**What would change my mind, cheapest first:**

1. **Read the band inside the loop.** One line in the admit and hire loops:
   read `.hud__refusal` immediately after each press and log it beside the
   command count. If the band is `not laid out` at a dropped press, the finding
   is *worse* than stated — a press that submits nothing and says nothing. If
   it carries the sentence, §1 narrows to "the sentence is unhelpful", which is
   a wording finding rather than a silence.
2. **Log `simulation/command-result` from the tee.** The tee already records
   every non-delta reply; filtering for `status: 'rejected'` and printing its
   `fault.code` would turn *"REASONED: a `past-tick` rejection desynced the
   sequence"* into a measurement. If no rejection appears, the mechanism is
   something else and §1's diagnosis is wrong while its symptom stands.
3. **Repeat act 4 at 1×.** If 25 presses land cleanly at 1× and lose seven at
   4×, the speed-scaled margin is confirmed. If they lose at both, the lead is
   not the cause.

**Second-weakest: §7's "nothing new arrived in the last three in-game days".**
Four identical alert readings across 6,300 ticks is solid evidence that no
*row* changed, and it is not evidence that no *incident opened* — a row already
carrying `7×` and `Day 14` would be updated in place if the coalescing keyed
on the same text, and I did not check whether the day stamp on a coalesced row
moves. The `0 · INCIDENTS · Clear` chip at each of those samples is consistent
with both readings.

**Third: §8's correction of my own first reading is right about construction
and I cannot fully account for the remainder.** 1,419 ticks for a perimeter is
MEASURED with the clock paused during drawing, and 59.1 against the code's 60
is as clean as this gets. But acts 1, 3 and 4 spent roughly 22,000 ticks
between `New prison` and a populated prison, and I have attributed the gap to
harness latency without measuring it directly. A run that logs the tick before
and after each individual instrument step would settle exactly how much of a
build is the game and how much is the tester.
