# West-facing cell doorway modules

The existing Blender wall kit supplies full and lowered doorframe collections; the existing open walnut door leaf supplies the hinge, panels, and hardware. This batch turns both collections 90° around the tile origin so the opening aligns with a west-edge wall. The full variant keeps the door leaf's authored height. The cutaway variant scales the leaf about its ground hinge to `0.52 / 2.28` of that height, matching the 0.52-tile lowered frame. The opening remains transparent between the jambs in both variants.

Logical IDs are `door.interior.open.west.full` and `door.interior.open.west.cutaway`. Each has 24 yaw poses at 15° steps and elevations 25°, 45°, and 65°. All transparent frames are 512 × 512 at 64 px/tile with pivot `(256, 256)`. The shared selector and lazy Phaser loader are unchanged. The existing registry producers preserve both new IDs on regeneration.

Blender 5.2.1 rerendered all 144 PNGs and two manifests with identical SHA-256 hashes. Full catalog payload is 1,220,639 bytes and cutaway payload is 814,735 bytes. At 1920 × 1080, the Phaser preview loaded only selected frames across five yaw values and both low and high elevations: full 11 images/189,041 bytes (including its initial selected pose), cutaway 10 images/111,188 bytes. Every image returned HTTP 200, and the pivot stayed at `(1370, 585)` under the pointer. Captures at yaw -45°, 0°, and 45° for elevations 25° and 65° are produced by `tooling/qa-oblique-west-door-browser.mjs`. Visual inspection confirms a tall framed opening and walnut leaf in the full variant, and low jambs plus a low leaf in the cutaway variant.

The registry test failed before the catalogs existed and passes after rendering. The pinned Blender live determinism gate passes. The separate ObliqueWorldScene painter must select these IDs for west-facing doors and choose the cutaway variant using its selection rule; this branch does not edit scene, world projector, camera, HUD, CI, or gameplay.
